Go Frog Go

A 16-bit endless road-crossing game. A frog played by Jev that starts knowing only "jump forward," taught by Nemotron after every death.

What got specific since scope. Scope said "Press play" and "Nemotron writes a lesson." Now we know it's one split screen, the frog moves on a five-move grid, the playbook starts as one line ("jump forward"), the coach only teaches after deaths and never moves the frog, and runs restart on their own until Stop.

THE SCREEN, STEP BY STEP

LOOK & FEEL

16-bit SNES style: rich palette, dark sprite outlines, shaded lower edges, textured two-tone grass. A frog. Colored cars on dark asphalt with yellow dashes. The right panel uses the same pixel font and palette so it reads as one game. Original sprites and name only. Picked by Matt from a live sample.

BEHAVIOR & HOW WE'LL KNOW

Endless road

Lanes generate ahead forever: grass strips and road runs, cars at varied speeds and directions. The frog moves on a grid: forward, left, right, back, wait.

  • Start sends the frog up the road with no human input
  • No finish line; the road keeps generating
  • A car touching the frog ends the run
Fast decisions (System 1 · Jev)

Each beat, Jev picks one of five moves from the nearby lanes, cars and the playbook, with a confidence. Jev makes every move in every run. Reached through a swappable adapter.

  • Every hop shows its move and confidence
  • With only "jump forward" in the playbook, the frog hops forward into traffic
Coach lessons (System 2 · after each death)

The coach never moves the frog. After a hit, Nemotron reads the last several moves and what hit the frog, and writes one lesson. Jev plays every later run with the whole playbook.

  • A new numbered lesson appears after each death
  • Lessons are specific to what happened, not generic
  • The next run visibly handles what caused the last death
Run history
  • Each run's distance is listed; best is highlighted
  • Over several runs, later distances are clearly higher than run 1
